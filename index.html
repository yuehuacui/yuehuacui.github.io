About me

I am a Professor in the Department of Statistics and Probability at Michigan State University.

My research interests include statistical genetics, statistical genomics, causal mediation analysis, multi-omics integration, and spatial transcriptomics. I also work on longitudinal and functional data analysis, mixture models, and statistical applications in the biological sciences.

I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from the University of Florida in 2005 and joined Michigan State University the same year. I was elected a Fellow of the American Statistical Association in 2022.

Research interests

  • Statistical genetics: genetic association studies, (e)QTL mapping, gene–gene and gene–environment interactions, Mendelian randomization, and high-dimensional mediation analysis.
  • Statistical genomics: single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and gene network inference.
  • Statistical methodology: longitudinal data analysis, functional data analysis, and mixture models.

Pancreatic cancer disparities research featured by Henry Ford Health

Henry Ford Health highlights our collaborative study in Cancer Research. Co-first authors Yuesong Wu and Aoqi Xie used spatial transcriptomics and multi-omics to identify differences in tumor subtypes and immune microenvironments between Black and non-Black pancreatic cancer patients. The research brings together Michigan State University, Henry Ford Health, and the University of Cincinnati.
